Minimum Viable Product Plan: An Insider Trading Whale Tracker for Prediction Markets
Section 1: Analysis of the Strategic Landscape: Identifying the Optimal Trading Venues
This section provides a foundational analysis of the prediction market landscape to identify the most suitable environment for the development and operation of the Insider Trading Whale Tracker MVP. The primary objective is to select a market that is not only operationally viable but also structurally aligned with the core strategy of identifying and tracking large, anonymous, and potentially informed traders. The analysis deconstructs the concept of "safety" into its constituent parts—regulatory stability, operational security, and strategic suitability—to arrive at a definitive recommendation.
1.1 The Paradox of "Safety": Deconstructing Risk in Prediction Markets
The selection of the "safest" market for this MVP presents a nuanced strategic choice. A superficial assessment might equate safety with the longest and most transparent history of regulatory compliance. However, for the specific purpose of tracking anonymous whales, the definition of safety is more complex. It involves a fundamental tension between formal regulatory oversight, which often mandates user identification and transparency, and the operational freedom afforded by platforms with a decentralized, privacy-preserving architecture.
The ideal environment for this MVP is one that balances two seemingly contradictory elements: sufficient regulatory legitimacy to ensure long-term operational viability in the target jurisdiction (the United States), and a platform structure that permits the anonymous trading activity that forms the basis of the whale-tracking thesis. Therefore, the "safest" market is not necessarily the most regulated in a traditional sense, but the one whose architecture and regulatory posture create a sustainable ecosystem for the desired trading signals to manifest.
This analysis reveals a duality in the concept of safety. One form of safety is top-down, derived from proactive engagement with federal regulators, which provides institutional confidence but may introduce state-level conflicts and limit user anonymity. Another form of safety is emergent, achieved through a strategic navigation of the regulatory landscape that preserves the core architectural benefits of a platform while securing a legal right to operate. This latter model, while potentially arising from a more tumultuous history, may ultimately offer a more stable and suitable environment for the individual trader executing this specific strategy.
1.2 Comparative Analysis: Kalshi - The Regulated Battleground
Kalshi represents the model of a proactively regulated financial exchange. From its inception, it sought and obtained a federal license from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), registering as a Designated Contract Market (DCM). This status provides a high degree of transparency and market integrity, making it an attractive venue for institutional participants and those seeking the protections afforded by a federally overseen market. Kalshi's contracts are designed to serve as tools for investors to hedge against financial impacts from real-world events, with position limits that can reach as high as $100 million on certain election markets.
However, this robust federal regulatory framework creates significant disadvantages for the whale tracker MVP. First, as a regulated financial exchange, Kalshi's operational model is antithetical to the user anonymity that is central to the strategy. The platform's structure is designed for compliance, which implicitly requires user identification, making it an inhospitable environment for the type of anonymous insider described in the source material.
Second, and more critically, Kalshi's federally-approved status has placed it in direct conflict with numerous state-level gaming regulators. Authorities in states like Nevada, New Jersey, and Massachusetts have challenged Kalshi's operations, asserting that its event contracts constitute unauthorized sports wagering or gambling under their respective state laws. This has resulted in a complex and ongoing legal battle centered on the question of whether the CFTC's federal authority preempts state gaming regulations. While Kalshi has secured important victories in federal court, which have affirmed that the legality of its contracts depends on the legality of the underlying event (e.g., elections, sporting events) rather than the act of wagering on it , the persistent opposition from state attorneys general creates a chaotic and unpredictable operating environment. This jurisdictional conflict poses a tangible risk to users, who could face sudden platform blockages or other enforcement actions depending on the outcome of litigation in their state of residence. This inherent instability makes Kalshi an unsuitable and high-risk choice for the MVP.
1.3 Comparative Analysis: Polymarket - The Resurgent Web3 Native
Polymarket offers a contrasting model that is far more aligned with the MVP's strategic objectives. Its architecture is fundamentally rooted in Web3 principles, operating as a decentralized application on the Polygon blockchain, an Ethereum Layer-2 solution. This structure allows users to interact with the platform via non-custodial Web3 wallets without undergoing Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures. This design choice is not merely a technical feature; it is the core enabler of the platform's value proposition as described in the source material. The transcript posits that prediction markets tacitly want informed "insiders" to trade on their platforms because this activity increases the accuracy and, therefore, the commercial value of their predictive data. An anonymous, low-friction environment is the ideal mechanism to attract such participants, who would be unwilling to trade on platforms that require real-world identity verification.
Polymarket's regulatory history has been more volatile than Kalshi's. The platform was fined $1.4 million by the CFTC in January 2022 for operating an unregistered platform and was required to block U.S. customers. In late 2024, its founder's home was raided by the FBI in connection with an investigation into election betting. However, the platform's trajectory has since shifted dramatically. Following a change in presidential administration that prioritized the digital asset industry, federal investigations by the Department of Justice and the CFTC were formally closed in July 2025.
Crucially, Polymarket then executed a strategic acquisition of QCEX, a CFTC-licensed derivatives exchange, for $112 million. This maneuver provided Polymarket with a Designated Contract Market (DCM) license, granting it a legal pathway to re-enter the U.S. market under full federal compliance. This strategic pivot allows Polymarket to offer the best of both worlds for the MVP: a federally permissible operating environment that mitigates the risk of a platform-wide shutdown, combined with a foundational Web3 user model that preserves the anonymity essential for the whale tracking strategy to function.
1.4 Recommendation: Polymarket as the MVP's Primary Target
Based on the preceding analysis, the definitive recommendation is to focus the MVP exclusively on the Polymarket platform. This conclusion is based on a synthesis of regulatory, operational, and strategic factors.
Kalshi, despite its strong federal regulatory standing, is an inappropriate venue. Its KYC-compliant, traditional exchange model is structurally misaligned with the goal of tracking anonymous whales. Furthermore, the significant and ongoing legal battles with state regulators introduce an unacceptable level of jurisdictional risk that could disrupt trading activity at any time.
Polymarket, conversely, is the natural habitat for the trading signals the MVP aims to detect. Its anonymous, non-custodial Web3 architecture creates the necessary conditions for "insider" activity to occur. The platform's recent resolution of federal regulatory issues and its acquisition of a CFTC license provide a sufficient degree of operational stability for a personal-use tool, effectively navigating the complex U.S. regulatory landscape to secure a legal path forward. The platform's API is also well-documented and provides the specific data endpoints required to identify user activity, trade sizes, and market impact, making it technically superior for this project's needs.

	Section 2: The Anatomy of a Market-Moving Whale: From Heuristics to High-Fidelity Signals
This section translates the qualitative description of an "insider trading whale" from the source material into a precise, quantitative set of rules that can be implemented in software. It establishes the logical core of the MVP by deconstructing the whale archetype, mapping its behavioral traits to specific, measurable data points available through the Polymarket API, and defining a concrete logic for generating actionable alerts.
2.1 Deconstructing the Whale Archetype
The source material provides a clear behavioral profile of the target entity. An "insider trading whale" on an illiquid prediction market is not simply a large trader, but one who exhibits a specific cluster of characteristics that, when taken together, suggest a high degree of confidence based on privileged information. The analysis of this archetype identifies four key traits that can serve as the basis for a detection algorithm:
1. New Accounts: Whales often operate through "completely new accounts" to obscure their activity or to operate with a clean slate. This suggests that the age of the trading account is a critical initial filter.
2. Confident, Huge Bets: They "confidently start making huge bets," indicating that the absolute size of a trade, measured in a stable currency like USDC, is a primary signal.
3. Pushing the Market: Their trading activity is significant enough to push "the whole market in that direction". This implies that a detectable signal is not just the size of the trade itself, but its immediate impact on the market's price.
4. High Success Rate: The trades associated with this activity pattern are observed to be "a winning trade over 50% of the time," distinguishing them from the random outcomes of a "degenerate gambler". While this is a lagging indicator best assessed over time, it validates the premise that the preceding characteristics are indicative of informed trading.
The MVP's logic will focus on the first three real-time, detectable signals—account age, trade size, and price impact—to generate immediate alerts, while the fourth—success rate—will be noted as a key metric for future enhancement and validation.
2.2 Mapping Heuristics to Polymarket API Data Points
The effectiveness of the MVP depends entirely on the ability to translate the abstract whale characteristics into concrete queries against the Polymarket Data API. The platform's API is uniquely suited for this task, offering endpoints that provide deep insight into user-level and market-level activity.
* Detecting "New Accounts": The heuristic of a new account can be precisely measured using the GET /activity endpoint. This endpoint provides a complete on-chain history for a given user address (proxyWallet), with each event having a timestamp. To determine an account's age, the MVP will, upon flagging a large trade, query this endpoint for the trader's proxyWallet and sort the results by timestamp in ascending order. The timestamp of the very first transaction will establish the account's creation date. An account will be flagged as "new" if its creation date falls within a configurable recent period (e.g., the last 30 days) relative to the time of the large trade.
* Detecting "Confident Huge Bets": This is the most straightforward signal to detect. The GET /trades endpoint returns a list of recent trades, with each trade object containing a size field. This field represents the value of the trade in USDC. The MVP will continuously poll this endpoint and apply a configurable size threshold (e.g., >$25,000 USDC) to identify trades that qualify as "huge." This threshold can be adjusted based on the overall liquidity of the market being monitored.
* Detecting "Pushing the Market": Measuring market impact requires a slightly more complex, two-step process using the GET /trades endpoint. When a huge bet is detected, the MVP must capture the state of the market immediately before and after the event. The price field in the trade object indicates the price at which the whale's trade was executed. To measure impact, the system will compare this execution price to the price of the most recent trade that occurred just prior to the whale's transaction. A significant change in price (e.g., >5%) in the direction of the whale's bet (e.g., a price increase on a BUY order) will serve as confirmation of market impact.
2.3 Defining the Whale Alert Logic
By synthesizing these detection mechanisms, the MVP can employ a multi-tiered alert system to differentiate between high-confidence signals and events that merely warrant further monitoring. This logic ensures that the user is notified of the most promising opportunities without being overwhelmed by lower-quality signals.
* Tier 1 Alert (High Priority): This is the primary alert and is triggered only when a single trade meets all three real-time criteria simultaneously. The logic is as follows:
   * Trade Size > Threshold_Size (e.g., 25,000 USDC)
   * AND Account Age < Threshold_Age (e.g., 30 days)
   * AND Price Impact > Threshold_Impact (e.g., 5%)
A Tier 1 alert represents the highest-confidence signal that a trade matches the full archetype of an informed insider as described in the source material. These alerts demand immediate attention and due diligence.
* Tier 2 Alert (Monitoring): This alert is triggered when a trade meets one or two of the primary criteria, suggesting potentially significant activity that does not fit the full profile. Examples include:
   * An extremely large trade from an established account (violates the "new account" rule but is still significant).
   * A market-moving trade from a new account that is below the "huge" size threshold (could be an insider testing the waters or operating with less capital).
The proxyWallet addresses associated with Tier 2 alerts will be added to a "watchlist." This allows the system to monitor their future activity more closely and potentially upgrade them to a higher priority if they exhibit a pattern of successful or market-moving trades over time.

	Section 3: The "Vibecoder's" Blueprint for an MVP Whale Tracker
This section provides a practical, step-by-step implementation guide for constructing the Insider Trading Whale Tracker. The blueprint is designed specifically for a "vibecoder"—a resourceful individual who, while not a professional programmer, is capable of connecting APIs and services to build functional tools, primarily through the use of AI code assistants and existing platform integrations. The architecture relies exclusively on free-tier services and the user's existing subscriptions to eliminate operational costs.
3.1 Conceptual Architecture: The Free-Tier Data Pipeline
The MVP's architecture is designed for simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and automation. It operates as a serverless data pipeline, where each component performs a specific task before passing data to the next. This model avoids the complexity and potential cost of managing a constantly running server, which is a key consideration. While a Replit subscription is available, its resources are best reserved for development, testing, and manual script execution. The production system leverages a serverless function, which only incurs costs during its brief execution period and fits comfortably within the generous free tiers offered by cloud providers.
The data flow is as follows:
1. Scheduler (GitHub Actions): A cron job, defined in a simple YAML file within a private GitHub repository, serves as the system's pacemaker. It triggers the entire process on a recurring schedule (e.g., every 15 minutes), ensuring consistent market monitoring without manual intervention.
2. Execution Engine (Google Cloud Functions): The GitHub Action makes a secure, authenticated HTTP request to trigger a serverless function hosted on Google Cloud. This Python-based function contains the core logic of the MVP: it fetches data, applies the whale detection rules, and formats the output.
3. Data Source (Polymarket API): The Cloud Function makes unauthenticated GET requests to the public Polymarket Data API endpoints (/trades and /activity) to retrieve the necessary raw data for analysis.
4. Logic Application: Within the Cloud Function, the Python script processes the JSON data returned by the API, applying the multi-tiered whale alert logic defined in Section 2.
5. Database & Dashboard (Google Sheets): When a whale alert is generated, the Cloud Function uses the Google Sheets API to write a new row containing the alert details (e.g., Wallet Address, Trade Size, Market Link, Timestamp) to a designated Google Sheet. This sheet serves as a simple, no-cost database and a real-time dashboard.
6. Alerting (Google Apps Script): An onEdit trigger within the Google Sheet, implemented with a few lines of Google Apps Script, detects when a new row is added by the Cloud Function. It then automatically sends a formatted email notification to the user, ensuring timely delivery of the intelligence.
3.2 Module 1: Data Acquisition with the Polymarket API
This module focuses on retrieving the necessary data from Polymarket. The script will primarily interact with the GET /trades endpoint to monitor for large transactions.
Instructions:
1. The core of this module is a Python function that uses the requests library to make an HTTP GET request.
2. The target URL will be https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades. To manage the data volume and focus on the most recent activity, use query parameters. For example: https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades?limit=100 will retrieve the last 100 trades.
3. The function must handle the JSON response, parsing it into a Python dictionary or list of dictionaries that can be iterated over in the next module.
4. Prompt for Google Jules: "Write a Python function named fetch_recent_trades that takes an integer limit as an argument. The function should use the requests library to make a GET request to https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades with the provided limit as a query parameter. It should handle potential errors and return the JSON response as a Python list of dictionaries."
3.3 Module 2: The Core Logic Engine in Google Cloud Functions
This module is the brain of the MVP. It will be deployed as a single Python script to a Google Cloud Function.
Instructions:
1. Setup: Create a new project in the Google Cloud Console. Navigate to Cloud Functions and create a new function. Choose Python as the runtime and select the "HTTP Trigger" option.
2. Code Implementation: The Python script for the function will import the fetch_recent_trades function from Module 1. It will then loop through each trade returned.
3. Whale Detection Logic:
   * Inside the loop, it will first check if trade['size'] exceeds the defined threshold (e.g., 25000).
   * If a trade is large enough, the script will extract the proxyWallet address and make a second API call to https://data-api.polymarket.com/activity?user={proxyWallet}&sortBy=TIMESTAMP&sortDirection=ASC&limit=1. This efficiently retrieves the very first activity for that user.
   * The script will calculate the account age by comparing the timestamp of this first activity to the current time.
   * It will then apply the full Tier 1 and Tier 2 alert logic defined in Section 2.3, checking trade size, account age, and price impact.
4. Output Formatting: For each trade that triggers an alert, the script will create a dictionary containing the key information: timestamp, wallet_address, trade_size, market_title, price, side, and a constructed URL to the Polymarket market page. These dictionaries will be collected into a list of alerts.
5. Prompt for Google Jules: "Using the fetch_recent_trades function, write a main Python function for a Google Cloud Function. This function should: a. Call fetch_recent_trades(100). b. Loop through each trade. If a trade's 'size' is greater than 25000: c. Make a new API call to the Polymarket /activity endpoint to get the first transaction for that trade's 'proxyWallet'. d. Check if the account is less than 30 days old. e. If it is, format the trade details into a dictionary and add it to a list called alerts. f. Return the alerts list."
3.4 Module 3: Data Persistence and Dashboarding in Google Sheets
This module connects the logic engine to a simple, effective database and dashboard.
Instructions:
1. API Setup: In your Google Cloud project, enable the "Google Sheets API" and the "Google Drive API". Create a Service Account under "IAM & Admin" -> "Service Accounts". Generate a JSON key for this service account and store it securely (it will be added as a secret in the Cloud Function environment).
2. Sheet Setup: Create a new Google Sheet. Share this sheet with the service account's email address, giving it "Editor" permissions.
3. Code Integration: Use a Python library like gspread to interact with the Google Sheets API. The Cloud Function script will need to be modified to authenticate using the service account credentials and open the designated sheet.
4. Data Writing: After the alerts list is generated, the script will loop through it and for each alert, it will call worksheet.append_row() to add the data to the Google Sheet.
5. Dashboarding: Use the built-in features of Google Sheets to enhance readability. Use "Conditional Formatting" to highlight new rows or color-code Tier 1 alerts in red for high visibility.
6. Prompt for Google Jules: "Add functionality to the previous Python script using the gspread library. The script should authenticate with a Google Cloud service account, open a Google Sheet by its name, select the first worksheet, and for every dictionary in the alerts list, it should append a new row to the sheet with the dictionary's values."
3.5 Module 4: Deployment and Alerting with GitHub Actions
This final module automates the entire process and sets up notifications.
Instructions:
1. Repository Setup: Create a new private repository on GitHub. Commit the Python script(s) and a requirements.txt file listing the necessary libraries (requests, gspread, etc.).
2. GitHub Actions Workflow: In the repository, create a directory path .github/workflows/ and add a new file named tracker.yml. This YAML file will define the workflow.
   * Trigger: Use the schedule trigger with a cron expression to run the job automatically. For example, schedule: - cron: '*/15 * * * *' will run it every 15 minutes.
   * Job Steps: The job will consist of steps to:
      * Check out the repository code.
      * Set up a Python environment and install the dependencies from requirements.txt.
      * Authenticate to Google Cloud using a GitHub Actions secret that stores your service account key.
      * Use the gcloud CLI or a simple curl command to invoke the HTTP trigger URL of your Google Cloud Function.
3. Alerting Script:
   * In your Google Sheet, go to "Extensions" -> "Apps Script".
   * Create a simple function that uses MailApp.sendEmail() to send a notification.
   * Create a trigger for this function by going to the "Triggers" section (clock icon) and setting it to run "From spreadsheet" and "On change". This ensures an email is sent every time the Cloud Function adds a new row.

	Section 4: Operationalizing the Intelligence: A Strategic Guide for Personal Trading
This final section transitions from the technical construction of the MVP to its practical application as a trading tool. It provides a strategic framework for interpreting the tracker's output, making informed trading decisions, managing inherent risks, and understanding the long-term viability of the strategy in an evolving market landscape.
4.1 From Alert to Action: A Trader's Workflow
The receipt of a Tier 1 alert from the MVP is not a directive to trade, but rather a high-priority signal to begin immediate due diligence. A disciplined workflow is essential to translate this raw signal into a well-reasoned trading decision.
1. Immediate Market Assessment: Upon receiving an alert, the first step is to open the specified market on the Polymarket platform. The alert provides the name and a direct link, minimizing search time.
2. Order Book Analysis: The next step is to analyze the market's central limit order book (CLOB). Confirm the whale's impact by observing the depth of the order book on both the "Yes" and "No" sides. A genuine market-moving trade will have visibly consumed a significant portion of the liquidity on one side, creating a price gap.
3. Liquidity Context: Assess the overall liquidity of the market. The source material notes that these patterns are most obvious in "big illiquid markets". If the market has extremely low volume, a large trade may have an outsized effect but could also be difficult to exit profitably. Conversely, in a highly liquid market, even a large trade may not be indicative of an information edge. The ideal scenario is a market with moderate liquidity where the whale's trade is significant but does not represent the entirety of the market's volume.
4. Strategic Entry and Exit: Based on the assessment, a trading decision can be made. If choosing to "piggyback" on the whale's trade, it is critical to define a clear plan. This includes determining an entry point (e.g., placing a limit order slightly below the current price), establishing a position size appropriate for the account's risk tolerance, and setting clear exit criteria. This should include both a take-profit level and, crucially, a stop-loss level to mitigate losses if the signal proves false.
4.2 Managing Risk: False Positives and Market Dynamics
The MVP is a probabilistic tool designed to increase the odds of identifying informed trading, but it is not infallible. Several scenarios can produce false positives, and effective risk management requires an awareness of these possibilities.
* Distinguishing Whales from Market Makers: A large trade may not be a speculative bet but rather the action of a designated market maker providing liquidity to both sides of the order book. Market makers' behavior is typically more systematic and less directional over time. To mitigate this, the MVP's Google Sheet database should include a separate tab for "Known Wallets." When a wallet is identified as a likely market maker or another non-insider entity (e.g., a known crypto fund), its address can be added to this list. The core logic in the Cloud Function can then be updated to ignore alerts from these addresses.
* The Uninformed Gambler: An alert could simply be a wealthy but uninformed gambler making a large, reckless bet. This is where the "new account" heuristic provides value, as insiders may prefer to use fresh wallets. However, the ultimate defense against this risk is disciplined position sizing and the use of stop-losses. No single alert should be trusted with an amount of capital that would result in a significant loss.
* The Primacy of User Judgment: The MVP is a signal generator, not an automated trading system. Its output is an input into a broader decision-making process that must include the user's own research, market understanding, and risk assessment. The tool enhances the trader's ability to spot opportunities but does not replace the need for critical judgment.
4.3 The Closing Window: The Future of the "Insider" Edge
A critical strategic consideration is the long-term viability of this trading approach. The opportunity the MVP seeks to exploit exists precisely because prediction markets currently occupy a "legal gray area" where certain activities are not explicitly regulated. However, this landscape is rapidly changing.
Polymarket's strategic decision to acquire a CFTC-licensed entity and re-enter the U.S. market as a regulated platform is a double-edged sword. While this move provides the operational stability necessary for the MVP to function today, it simultaneously signals the beginning of the end for the very regulatory ambiguity that creates the "insider" edge.
As Polymarket matures under the oversight of the CFTC, it will inevitably face increasing pressure to implement more robust market surveillance and compliance policies. The CFTC's mandate is to ensure fair and orderly markets and to prevent manipulation. While Polymarket's business model may benefit from the predictive accuracy provided by informed traders, its new status as a regulated entity will force it to prioritize market integrity to maintain its license. The delicate balance the platform currently maintains between tacitly wanting informed traders and appearing to have a fair market will be forced to shift decisively toward the latter. This will likely lead to the implementation of stricter rules, more sophisticated monitoring for manipulative trading patterns, and potentially even the introduction of position limits or other controls that would diminish the effectiveness of the whale-tracking strategy. Therefore, traders employing this strategy should be aware that the window of opportunity is likely finite and will narrow as the regulatory framework matures.
4.4 Post-MVP Roadmap: Enhancing the Tracker
To maintain an edge as the market evolves, the MVP should be viewed as a foundation for continued development. A potential roadmap for future enhancements includes:
* Whale Profit & Loss (P&L) Tracking: Implement a system to track the historical performance of flagged whale wallets. By recording the outcome of the markets they trade in, the system can calculate a win/loss record and P&L for each whale. This data can be used to assign a "confidence score" to alerts, prioritizing notifications from historically profitable wallets.
* Multi-Whale Confirmation (Cluster Detection): Develop logic to detect when multiple, independent, and previously flagged "whale" wallets are all trading on the same side of the same market within a short period. This "cluster" activity would represent a significantly stronger signal than a trade from a single whale.
* Advanced User Interface (UI): While Google Sheets is an effective and free MVP dashboard, a more sophisticated UI could provide greater analytical power. Using a framework like Streamlit or Replit's web hosting capabilities, a simple web application could be built to visualize whale activity over time, chart market impact, and manage watchlists more effectively.
* Market Condition Filtering: Add logic to filter alerts based on market characteristics. For example, the user could configure the tracker to only monitor markets with a specific range of trading volume or to ignore certain categories of markets (e.g., sports) to focus on others (e.g., corporate earnings, geopolitical events).
